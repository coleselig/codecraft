Wild Love Bakehouse

We read through wildlovebakehouse.com on November 18, 2026, the way someone planning a trip up Central for croissants would. You own a short, easy-to-remember address, and your holiday notice gives a clear pickup day and time window. Here are a few things that would help the site work as hard as your ovens do.

What we found

Each item below is something we saw ourselves on the live site today. Most are quick fixes.

1The “www” version of your address shows a different, older page

What we saw

wildlovebakehouse.com shows your holiday pre-order notice, but www.wildlovebakehouse.com shows a different page that says We are coming back!, Curbside pickup style! April 25! and asks for help through a Virtual Tip Jar for an emergency relief fund.

Why it matters

Many people type “www” out of habit. They land on a page that sounds like the bakery is closed or still reopening, and may not make the trip.

Quick fix

In your website or domain settings, point the “www” address to the same page as the main address, and remove the old reopening and tip-jar page.

2The site says your hours changed, but doesn't list them

What we saw

The page opens with We have updated our hours of operation. The only times on the page are the holiday pickup window, Thursday 12/24, 8am-4pm. Your regular days and hours aren't on either version of the site.

Why it matters

“When are you open?” is the first question people bring to a bakery's website, especially one that sells out. Telling them the hours changed without saying what they are can leave them guessing.

Quick fix

Add your weekly hours right under that line, matching your Google listing, plus a short note that you close early when the case sells out.

3There's no address, phone number or directions

What we saw

Neither page shows your street address (1625 N Central St), your phone number ((865) 200-8078) or a map link. We didn't find any links on the site at all.

Why it matters

Someone who found you through a “best bakery” article and opens your site can't tap to call or get directions from it, and has to go back and search again.

Quick fix

Add your address with a Google Maps link and your phone number as a tap-to-call (tel:) link, near the top of the page.

4The pre-order notice doesn't say how to pre-order, or which year it's for

What we saw

The notice says Holiday Pre-Orders are open until Tuesday 12/22 at noon or till we sell out. There's no phone number, order form, email or link to place an order, and the dates don't include a year.

Why it matters

A customer ready to order a holiday pie has no next step on the page, and without a year they can't tell whether the notice is for this season or a past one.

Quick fix

Add one line such as “Call (865) 200-8078 to pre-order” (or a link to an order form), and include the year in the dates, or take the notice down after the holidays.

5No menu, and no link to your Facebook page

What we saw

The site mentions seasonal pastries, croissants, and grab and go pies, but there's no menu or list of what you usually bake, and no link to your Facebook page (facebook.com/wildlovebakehouse), which is listed for you in local directories.

Why it matters

People like to know what they might find before they come. If your daily updates live on Facebook, a link sends visitors straight to them.

Quick fix

Add a short “usually in the case” list (croissants, pies and tarts, cakes, cookies, coffee) and a Facebook button that points to your page.

What's working

  • You own your name online. wildlovebakehouse.com is short, matches your business name and is easy to say out loud or print on a bag.
  • Your holiday notice is specific. It gives a cut-off (Tuesday 12/22 at noon), a pickup day and a time window, which is exactly the detail people want.
  • It sounds like you. The warm, plain wording, like Thank you!, reads like a neighborhood bakery, not a template.
