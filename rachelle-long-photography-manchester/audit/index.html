Rachelle Long Photography

We read through rlongphotos.com, the website your booking page links to, on November 18, 2026, page by page, the way a parent looking for a newborn photographer would. Most of what we found isn't really a design problem: the site doesn't look like it's yours anymore. Here's what we saw and what we'd do about it.

Checked November 18, 20265 findingsNo cost, no strings

What we found

Each item below is something we saw ourselves on the live site today.

1The contact details are template placeholders

What we saw

The contact section reads 123-456-7890 and 123 Main Street, Anytown, USA. The FAQ repeats them: The phone number for Rachelle Long Photography is 123-456-7890.

Home page: rlongphotos.com (contact section and FAQ)
Why it matters

A parent wanting to book a newborn session can't call or find you from this page, and nothing on it says you're in Manchester, Tennessee.

Easy fix

Take the link to rlongphotos.com off your booking page for now, so clients go straight to a place where your real details are.

2The photos are stock images, not your work

What we saw

The home page images are files named pexels-photo-4014942, pexels-photo-3773770, pexels-photo-4008890, pexels-photo-260024 and pexels-photo-3491697, which is how photos from the free stock library Pexels are named. None of them have descriptions.

Home page: rlongphotos.com
Why it matters

For a photographer the photos are the whole pitch. Clients choose you by your style, and stock images don't show them that.

Easy fix

Lead any site with 10–15 of your own favorite sessions: newborns, smash cakes and seniors.

3Another photographer's name is on your Contact page

What we saw

The Contact page shows three articles, one of them titled Contact Us - Fizzah Raza Photography (dated Dec 10, 2019). The page has no phone number, email or contact form.

Contact page: rlongphotos.com/category/contact
Why it matters

A client who goes looking for how to reach you finds a different business's name and no way to get in touch.

Easy fix

Your contact page should hold one thing: how to book you (your Square calendar link, email and phone).

4The site has ads and articles that have nothing to do with you

What we saw

The home page shows a promo, Free AI API with no key: copy the ready-made integration prompt, plus guides such as How Beginners Can Use Unfiltered AI in Professional Photography. The footer reads (с) Copying without a direct dofollow link is a violation of copyright.

Home page: rlongphotos.com
Why it matters

Clients reading this under your name could easily think you wrote or endorse it.

Easy fix

See the next item. This kind of content usually means someone else is now running the address.

5The web address may not be in your hands anymore

What we saw

The public domain registration record shows rlongphotos.com was registered on September 22, 2023 (registrar: NameSilo), after the posts the site shows dated June 26, 2021 and July 28, 2023.

Public registration (RDAP) record for rlongphotos.com
Why it matters

When a domain lapses, anyone can register it and fill it with content under your old business name, which matches what's on the site now. If you didn't re-register it yourself in September 2023, the people running this site aren't you, and your booking page is sending clients to them.

Easy fix

Check whether you have a NameSilo account or renewal emails for rlongphotos.com. If you don't, remove the link everywhere and choose a new address for your own site.

What's working

  • Clients can still book you. The issue is only the link. Your booking calendar is a solid place to send people once a real site points to it.
  • Your name is easy to find. “Rachelle Long Photography” is distinctive, so a site of your own with your real details has a good shot at being what people find when they look you up.
  • Your sessions are easy to explain. Newborn, smash cake and senior sessions are clear, specific offers that are easy to show off with a handful of your own photos.
