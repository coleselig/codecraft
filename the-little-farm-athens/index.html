Inspired by single-barrel scotch

Every hive tastes a little different.

On any given day, 10,000 to 20,000 bees leave a hive to forage, dancing directions to the best nectar back at home. Two hives side by side can work completely different blooms, like blueberries or sunflowers, and the honey shows it.

That's why we never blend. Each hive is harvested, processed and bottled separately, and every bottle carries the name of the queen whose colony made it. Visit the farm and you can taste them side by side.

Why folks buy from us

A family farm, farmed for the long haul.

Annie and her family have farmed in East Tennessee for a long time. Here's what makes The Little Farm, well, ours.

Gentle on the land

Minimal pesticides and synthetic chemicals, crop rotation, cover crops and integrated pest management, all to build healthier soil every year.

A farm with a story

We farm the former buffalo farm of Dr. Davis: the original 1942 farmhouse plus five acres of pasture, berry garden, apiary, vegetable garden and orchard.

Gentle bees, honest honey

Raised with help from a mentor and the Cherokee Beekeepers Association, our bees are calm and our honey is never blended between hives.

Hens that retire here

We never harvest our birds. When a hen slows down or stops laying, she stays with the flock and rests.

Hand-combed fiber

Every fleece is shorn, picked clean, washed, dried and hand-combed to keep only the finest, longest fiber.

Affordable & local

Our goal is simple: fresh eggs, honey and fruit at prices our Athens neighbors can enjoy, for generations to come.

Meet the residents

The flock, the herd & the queen

The orchard hens

About 25 chickens patrol the orchard for bugs and lay our eggs. Most are pets who'll happily take a neck scratch. There's no rooster (and we like it that way). The flock is kept safe and orderly by a benevolent hen named Queenie.

  • Black Marans
  • Cochins
  • Leghorns
  • Wyandottes
  • Barred Rocks
  • Brahmas
  • Sussex
  • Australorps

Goats & sheep

Our fiber comes from Angora goats (mohair), Pygmy-Angora crosses (finer, cashmere-quality fiber) and Shetland sheep, a small, ancient breed from the Shetland Islands with very fine wool in natural colors. The goats are gentle and mellow, and big fans of tortilla chips.

  • Angora goats
  • Pygmy-Angora cross
  • Shetland sheep
  • Goats shorn twice a year
  • Sheep shorn each spring

Lambs and kids are sometimes available after weaning (4+ months). Email Annie to ask.
